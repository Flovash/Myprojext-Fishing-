# Plan: The director's toolkit

Status: idea stage (2026-10-18). Nothing built yet. Builds on PLAN_rituals.md and what the game already does.

The goal: give the director tools to shape each wave (what's on screen, how swingy it feels, when the table runs hot or dry) so players get surprises, while the bank always comes back to its target (97-98%) over time.

---

## 1. First, the rituals: fewer, and natural

24 rituals is too many. In a real ocean only a few things are big enough to move everything. Keep **6 rituals**; the other fish behaviors stay as **reactions** (a fish swerving around ink, clownfish guarding a seat) that happen on their own and don't need the whole table.

The game already has most of them as **scenes** (each scene sets which fish come and a payback factor):

| Ritual | Already in game as | Natural? | What it gives the director |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bait Ball** | scene "Bait Ball" (lots of tiny fish, payback ×1.06) | Yes, happens every day at sea | A **payback moment**: many easy small wins for everyone |
| **Feeding Frenzy** | (new) | Yes | A **swing moment**: big fish, big wins or big misses |
| **The Current** | scene "The Current" (×0.98) | Yes | A calm, even stretch |
| **Turtle Parade / Cleaning Station** | scene "Turtle Parade" (lots of mediums) | Yes | A **medium wave** that looks natural |
| **Night Water / Coral Spawning** | scene "Night Water" | Yes, a few nights a year | Small and odd fish; a change of mood |
| **Make Way** | boss entrances | Yes | The boss moment |

The change from today: instead of the director just switching scenes, a ritual **starts when routes line up** (sardines circling + a second school joining = Bait Ball). The director can still decide whether to let it happen (the gates and chance roll).

---

## 2. The rule that makes all of this safe

In this game a fish's catch chance is **its payback ÷ its multiplier**. A 2× fish is easy to catch; a 300× fish is hard. That means **every shot returns the same on average, whatever fish it hits and whatever the bet**: about 97 points back per 100 bet.

That answers your worry about the 100-bet player:
- If a 100-bet player shoots a school of easy fish, the bank does **not** lose on average. Easy fish pay little. Each shot still returns about 97 of 100.
- What the bank does take is **swing**: a 100-bet player who hits a lucky streak moves the bank a lot in a short time. So the risk to watch is **swing, not average**, and swing comes back to normal on its own.

So the director has three kinds of levers:

| Lever | What it changes | Risk to the bank |
|---|---|---|
| **What's on screen** (fish, formations) | How the wave looks and feels | None |
| **How swingy** (easy vs hard fish, moods) | How often players win, and how big | Short-term swing only |
| **How much** (payback above or below target for a while) | Real ups and downs: dry spells and hot streaks | Real, so it must always be paid back |

Only the third lever really moves money, so it gets the strictest rules (section 4).

## 3. The tools

### Tool 1: Wave recipes (your "medium wave" idea)
A recipe says how much of each group comes this wave. Examples:

| Recipe | Tiny | Small | Medium | Big | Bosses | Feels like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Small rain** | lots | lots | few | none | off | Constant small wins |
| **Medium wave** | some | some | **lots** | few | off | Steady mid-size wins |
| **The hunt** | few | few | some | **lots** | off | Long waits, big hits |
| **Mixed reef** | normal | normal | normal | normal | maybe | The default |

Scenes already do this with their fish mix. The new part: the director picks a recipe on purpose to steer the wave, and the lab measures what each recipe does.

### Tool 2: Formation decks (same fish, different looks)
So a medium wave doesn't look like "tuna, tuna, tuna", each fish gets a **deck of formations with percentages**, and the recipe can change the percentages. Example for a medium wave:
- **Tuna:** arc 30%, echelon 25%, line 25%, ball 20%. Never the same formation twice in a row.
- **Barracuda:** loose 60%, pair 40%.
- **Turtle:** single 70%, slow pair 30%.

More of the same fish in different shapes and routes looks natural. Formations and weights per season already exist (Tuna has 4 formations); this makes them a director tool per wave.

### Tool 3: Soft and hard schools (your "70% easy to hit" idea)
A school can be **soft** (most fish are low-multiplier and easy to hit: lots of small wins) or **hard** (fewer, higher-multiplier fish: rare but big wins). Because of the rule in section 2, both return the same on average. Only how it *feels* changes.
- **To build players up slowly:** soft schools, so wins come often and credits drift gently.
- **To let things cool down:** hard schools, so wins come less often but there's a chance of a big one.
- **The game has the start of this already:** each group has a "stable" or "swingy" mood that changes every 1-3 minutes.

Soft schools alone can't drain a player. Draining only comes from payback below target (Tool 4), and that has to be paid back.

### Tool 4: The payback tide (the uneven filter that comes back to square)
The director runs the table along a **tide**: payback goes up and down from wave to wave, but a **tide ledger** keeps count of every point above or below 97.5%, and the ledger must come back to zero within a set time (for example 60-90 minutes).

Tide shapes the director can choose:

| Shape | Looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat** | 97.5% every wave | Stable, nothing surprising |
| **Rolling** | 94 → 101 → 95 → 100 | Gentle ups and downs |
| **Save and spike** | 93, 94, 94, then **112** | A quiet stretch saves up for one big moment (a Bait Ball, a boss) |
| **Storm** | 88, 109, 91, 106 | Wild swings, rarely used |

**The 2, 3 and 4-way split.** The tide is split into channels, each with its own share, all adding up to the target:
- **2-way:** sardine vs minnow (the seesaw, already built: one runs hot while the other runs cold, and they average out)
- **3-way:** gun / pole / golden pocket
- **4-way:** steady base / golden pocket (saved for the golden fish and koi moments) / ritual pool (pays for Bait Ball moments) / swing pool (hot and cold seesaws)

The golden pocket already works like this: a small share of every bet goes into it, and it pays out in golden moments. This makes it a general system.

### Tool 5: Planned dry spells (different kinds)
A dry spell is the tide below target, on purpose, to save up for something creative later. Each kind has a different feel and a hard limit:

| Dry spell | What players see | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Calm water** | Fewer fish, slower, quiet | Up to 2 waves |
| **The long hunt** | Big fish everywhere but hard to land | Up to 1 wave |
| **Thin schools** | Normal look, but the soft fish are rarer | Up to 2 waves |

Rules: no seat goes longer than a set dry limit (for example 75 s without a win), and every dry spell must be followed by a payback moment (save and spike).

### Tool 6: Tracking by bet size and time (your 1-token point)
You can't steer what you can't see. The director tracks:
- **Payback by bet size:** 1 token, 2-9, 10-49, 50+. If 1-token players are getting drained, it shows straight away.
- **By time window:** last minute, last 10 minutes, session, night.
- **Swing exposure right now:** how much the bank could pay out in the next few seconds if the big bettors hit what they're aiming at. When it's high, the director holds back spikes and soft schools in front of those seats.

**For 1-token players:** they should always have something to shoot. Every wave keeps some easy small fish within reach of every seat, so a 1-token player gets regular wins even during a dry spell.

### Tool 7: The risk check before a big moment
Before the director sends a spike (a Bait Ball at 1.06, a golden moment), it checks: is the tide ledger and golden pocket full enough to pay for this if it goes well for the players? If not, it waits or picks a smaller moment.

---

## 4. Guardrails

1. **The long run is fixed.** Payback comes back to target (97.5% on normal); every dip is paid back inside the tide window. The lab proves it every run.
2. **Table-wide, not aimed at one player.** The director may use a seat's data to help a cold seat (the cold-seat plays already do this), but never to drain one particular player.
3. **Movement still never changes chances.** Routes and formations only decide where and when fish swim. Chances change only through the payback layer (tide, moods, soft and hard schools), where the lab can measure it.
4. **Caps everywhere.** Maximum dry time per seat, maximum spike size, maximum swing exposure.
5. **If this ever runs for real money or prizes:** games that adjust payouts based on past results are regulated or banned in many places. Fine for points-only play; check the local rules before money is ever involved.

## 5. What the lab adds
- Every tool's decisions logged per wave (recipe, formations, tide shape, dry spell type), and shown in the replay's **Jump to** list.
- New report sections: the tide ledger over the night (does it come back to square?), payback by bet size, the longest dry spell per seat, how 1-token players did.
- The test bench compares tool settings side by side in every table setup.
